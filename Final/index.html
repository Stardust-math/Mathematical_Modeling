Abstract

This project studies free riding in dynamic public-good provision. Public goods such as environmental governance, open-source maintenance, public infrastructure, and community services create a common governance dilemma: the collective stock benefits all participants, while contribution costs are borne privately. To explain how decentralized individual rationality can generate persistent under-provision, the project develops a simulation-based Dynamic Stock–Pressure Free-Riding framework. The model combines heterogeneous agents, a dynamic public-good stock, maintenance pressure, demand feedback, capacity saturation, and policy intervention. Controlled synthetic scenarios are used to compare a Nash-style individual-rational benchmark with a stage-wise social-planner benchmark, quantify free-riding gaps and welfare losses, and evaluate subsidy, penalty, reputation, matching fund, threshold governance, and combined portfolio policies under sensitivity, robustness, Monte Carlo, and Pareto trade-off analyses.
